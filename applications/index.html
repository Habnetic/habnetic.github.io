From research to practice

Can you rely on decisions informed by uncertain models?

A precise-looking model output does not necessarily lead to a reliable decision.

Engineers, planners and researchers use model results to select designs, allocate inspections or decide where to collect more evidence. Limited budgets and uncertain inputs can turn apparently clear rankings into difficult choices.

Habnetic asks which choices remain stable under plausible model outcomes, which depend strongly on assumptions, and what evidence would be needed to trust them. Its published experiment answers a narrower version of this question for fixed-capacity urban flood-risk prioritisation.

Habnetic complements specialist models and simulations; it does not replace them or claim that the proposed wider applications have already been validated.

The decision problem

A ranking is not the whole decision

Consider a municipality with thousands of buildings but capacity to investigate only a small proportion. A risk model produces a ranking and a selection is made. The first Habnetic study asks what happens to that selection as the model estimates vary. Other decisions may have different constraints and consequences.

01 · Estimate

Model the risk

Available indicators and model assumptions generate estimates that are subject to uncertainty.

02 · Select

Choose a priority set

Limited capacity turns a continuous ranking into a discrete choice: inside or outside the selected set.

03 · Question

Would the selection change?

Some assets remain consistently selected. Others may change status as model parameters vary.

This three-step diagram describes the published flood-risk experiment. Decision stability is measurable under a fitted model, but stability alone does not establish whether the evidence is adequate, the ranking is empirically correct or the consequences of a wrong choice are acceptable.

What Habnetic investigates

What the first experiment actually measures

Instead of selecting assets once from a single ranking, the research ranks and selects the top k assets for each posterior model draw. The proportion of draws in which an asset is selected estimates its posterior top-k membership probability.

High membership

Consistently selected

Assets with a posterior top-k inclusion probability of at least 0.8 under the paper's primary classification convention.

Intermediate membership

Uncertain selection

Assets with inclusion probabilities between 0.2 and 0.8. These constitute the uncertain decision boundary.

Low membership

Consistently excluded

Assets with inclusion probabilities of at most 0.2 under the same classification convention.

These classification thresholds are analytical choices, and their sensitivity is assessed in the paper. Membership probabilities are conditional on the model and data. They are not probabilities of flooding or physical damage.

Research evidence

Three cities, one experimental specification

The published experiments cover a Rotterdam baseline, controlled hazard-proxy perturbations and independent model fits under a common specification in Hamburg and Donostia–San Sebastián. This is cross-city stress testing, not probabilistic knowledge transfer between cities.

Rotterdam

30 assets

A narrow membership boundary

At approximately 1% prioritisation capacity, only 30 of 221,324 buildings fall within the uncertain top-k membership category. A separate perturbation experiment involving 50,000 assets shows limited broadening of this boundary under the tested conditions.

Hamburg

56 assets

Fixed-specification stress test

At comparable prioritisation capacity, 56 of 341,530 buildings fall within the uncertain membership category. The model was fitted independently using the same specification and Rotterdam-based reference scaling.

Donostia–San Sebastián

13 assets

Capacity gives the count context

Only 13 of 7,755 buildings fall within the uncertain membership category. However, this represents 16.67% of the prioritisation capacity (k = 78), compared with just 1.36% in Rotterdam and 1.64% in Hamburg.

Research note: Uncertain membership refers to posterior top-k inclusion probabilities strictly between 0.2 and 0.8 under the tested model. It does not represent uncertainty in observed flood damage.

The maps visualise model-derived selection membership, not observed flood damage or validated hydraulic risk. See the published research preprint for methods and experimental limitations.

Potential applications

Where might the research approach be tested next?

These are potential research domains, not validated applications or existing products. Each would need its own decision rule, uncertainty model, empirical checks and evidence of usefulness. Flood-risk prioritisation remains the established experimental case.

Candidate domain · Building simulation

Building performance and design decisions

Explore how choices between design, retrofit or investment options change across uncertain climate, energy and comfort simulations. The research question concerns the decision, not a replacement for the simulation software.

Candidate domain · Earth observation

Satellite observation planning

Investigate how limited observation capacity could be allocated when information is incomplete: which places or times might deserve attention, and how sensitive would those priorities be to assumptions?

First experimental domain

Urban flood-risk and resilience assessment

Study the stability of fixed-capacity selections from uncertain estimates. Work in Phase 4 aims to improve topographic and hazard evidence, exposure, consequences and independent validation.

Potential extension · Portfolios

Infrastructure and asset management

Explore how uncertainty affects inspection, maintenance or assessment priorities across large portfolios with limited capacity.

Potential extension · Planning

Urban and regional planning

Examine whether selected sites or interventions remain defensible as indicators, constraints, assumptions and available evidence change.

Proposed Phase 5 · Transfer

Knowledge transfer to data-scarce settings

Test whether probabilistic information learnt in data-rich settings can assist decisions elsewhere using hierarchical models and partial pooling, while checking for incompatible evidence and negative transfer.

Research maturity

What has been tested, and what remains open?

Habnetic is independent open-source research. Published evidence currently concerns selection stability in deliberately simplified flood-risk experiments. Generalisation, field validation and probabilistic transfer are future research tasks.

Demonstrated experimentally

  • Posterior top-k membership probabilities and decision-stability classes.
  • Controlled perturbations of the hazard proxy.
  • Fixed-specification cross-city stress testing with independent city fits.
  • Open research code and reproducible experimental inputs.

Further validation needed

  • Observed flood impacts or losses and independent validation (Phase 4).
  • More realistic topographic, hazard, exposure and vulnerability evidence (Phase 4).
  • Prior, likelihood and model sensitivity; consequences of wrong choices.
  • Independent testing in another decision domain (Phase 4), followed by explicit knowledge-transfer tests (proposed Phase 5).

Current published experiments use simplified exposure and hazard proxies and synthetic binary outcomes. They do not validate building-level flood-loss predictions or the empirical correctness of selected priorities. Phase 3 fitted cities independently; no posterior knowledge was transferred. Phase 4 improvements and Phase 5 transfer research are not completed validations or operational products.

The researcher behind Habnetic

Who is behind Habnetic?

Habnetic was founded by Mikel Martínez Mugica, an architect and independent researcher based in Zürich. His background combines built projects, university teaching, computational design and probabilistic modelling. Habnetic begins with decisions in the built environment while investigating methods that may generalise beyond it.

Explore a research collaboration

Could this approach be relevant to your decisions?

Habnetic welcomes research conversations with specialists in probabilistic modelling, building simulation, Earth observation, urban risk, engineering, planning and decision analysis.

Useful collaborations would start with a real decision problem: its constraints, uncertain model outputs, available evidence and the consequences of mistakes. The aim is to design a falsifiable test, not to assume that the existing flood-risk results automatically apply elsewhere.
